# Germany, Austria, Liechtenstein and the former German east

Partly adapted from de-ahnenforschung-skills (Schrauberhirn / Hermes Agent community, MIT). For Switzerland read switzerland.md.

## Germany
**Civil registration (Standesamt)**: Prussia from 1 October 1874, the whole German Empire from 1 January 1876. Earlier in the Rhineland and other areas under French rule (from 1798/1810), and in Baden (1870). The record stays at the Standesamt of the place of the event until the access period ends, then it moves to the municipal or state archive.
**Closure periods (Personenstandsgesetz §5)**: birth registers 110 years, marriage registers 80 years, death registers 30 years. Before that only persons with a legal interest (for example direct descendants with proof) receive copies.
**Church books (Kirchenbücher)**: before civil registration nearly the only source; from the 16th–17th century, many lost in the Thirty Years War. Separate books for Protestant (evangelisch), Catholic and Jewish communities.
- **Archion** (archion.de): Protestant church books, paid subscription.
- **Matricula** (data.matricula-online.eu): Catholic church books of many dioceses (Passau, Münster, Paderborn, Hildesheim, Mainz, Köln parts and more), free. No bulk downloads.
- **FamilySearch**: many filmed church books and civil registers.
- Diocesan and regional church archives hold what is not online.
**Community sources**
- **GenWiki / CompGen** (wiki.genealogy.net, compgen.de): place articles with the responsible parishes and registries, Ortsfamilienbücher (online-ofb.de), address books, WWI Verlustlisten (des.genealogy.net).
- **Ortsfamilienbuch (OFB)**: a village reconstructed family by family from church books; excellent lead, check against the book.
**20th century**
- WWI: Prussian and other Verlustlisten 1914–1919 (index on des.genealogy.net).
- WWII Wehrmacht: the former Deutsche Dienststelle (WASt) is now part of the Bundesarchiv (Abteilung PA); requests by relatives. Volksbund Gräbersuche for the fallen.
- Expellees from the east: Lastenausgleichsarchiv (Bayreuth, Bundesarchiv), Heimatortskarteien (HOK) of the churches' tracing services, Standesamt I in Berlin for registers from the former eastern territories that came to Germany.
- Emigration: Hamburg and Bremen passenger lists; Auswanderer databases of the German Emigration Center Bremerhaven.
- Address books (Adressbücher) of cities are often digitised by state libraries.
**Former eastern territories (Silesia, Pomerania, East and West Prussia, Posen)**: church books and civil registers are mostly in Polish (szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl), Russian (Kaliningrad) or Lithuanian (Memel) archives today; some at the Evangelisches Zentralarchiv Berlin.

## Austria
- Church books are the civil record until 1938 (civil registration introduced only in 1938). Many Austrian dioceses are on **Matricula** (Vienna, Lower Austria/St. Pölten, Linz, Salzburg, Graz-Seckau, Gurk, Passau parts), free.
- Jewish records: IKG Wien and **GenTeam** (genteam.at, free registration) indexes.
- **Austrian State Archives / Kriegsarchiv** (oesta.gv.at): Austro-Hungarian army conscription records (Grundbuchblätter), officers' files.
- **ANNO** (anno.onb.ac.at): Austrian newspapers, including the Austro-Hungarian Verlustlisten 1914–1919.
- Vienna City and State Archives (WStLA, WAIS catalogue): Meldezettel (residence registration) for Vienna, probate records.
- Austrian families often came from Bohemia, Moravia, Galicia, Hungary: see czech-slovak.md and poland-baltics.md.

## Liechtenstein
Liechtensteinisches Landesarchiv (e-archiv.li); parish books of the Catholic parishes.

## Reading the records
Older German records are in Kurrent (German cursive) or Sütterlin, Latin in Catholic books. Write the reading as given and mark uncertain letters.

## Requests
Standesamt of the place of the event (or the archive after the closure period): request a "beglaubigter Ausdruck aus dem Geburtenregister" or a copy; give name, date, place, parents, and your relationship with proof. Church archives answer by e-mail, usually paid. Bundesarchiv personal records: online form on bundesarchiv.de.
