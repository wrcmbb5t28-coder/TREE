# Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, Galicia and Bukovina

Adapted from genealogy-research by chocianowicz (MIT) and ex-ussr-kinship-research by Denis Shiryaev (MIT). For Belarus and Ukraine also read ex-ussr.md.

## Which state, which archive
Polish lands were split 1795–1918 between Russia (Congress Kingdom, Lithuanian and Belarusian governorates), Prussia (Posen, West Prussia, Silesia, Pomerania) and Austria (Galicia). Interwar Poland 1918–1939 included today's western Ukraine, western Belarus and the Vilnius region. Borders moved again in 1945. Always decide the archive by the state and district at the date of the event, not by today's country.

## Record types
- Catholic, Greek Catholic, Lutheran, Orthodox and Jewish parish/civil-religious books. In the Congress Kingdom (Russian Poland) from 1808 the parish priest was also the civil registrar ("akta stanu cywilnego"); there are often two copies, one in the parish and one in the state archive.
- In Prussian areas civil registry offices (Standesamt) from October 1874; registers are mostly in Polish state archives today, some in Germany (see german-speaking.md).
- In Galicia church books (Latin and Polish forms) from 1784 under Austrian rules; Greek Catholic for most Ukrainian rural families, Roman Catholic for Poles and Germans, Jewish books separate.
- Revision lists and estate inventories (inwentarze) in Lithuanian and Belarusian lands; nobility (szlachta) proofs before heraldry commissions.
- Austrian cadastres (Josephine 1785–1789, Franciscan 1819–1820) for Galicia and Bukovina.
- Austro-Hungarian military: Verlustlisten 1914–1919, conscription records (Grundbuchblätter) in the Vienna War Archive (see german-speaking.md).

## Key online sources
- **szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl**: scans and catalogues of the Polish State Archives, free. Search by place, then open "akta stanu cywilnego" for the parish.
- **Geneteka** (geneteka.genealodzy.pl): the largest index of Polish parish records, also sections for Belarus, Ukraine and Lithuania; index only, open the scan to confirm.
- **metryki.genealodzy.pl**: volunteer scans of parish books.
- **Polona** (polona.pl) and regional digital libraries: the Słownik geograficzny Królestwa Polskiego (gazetteer for every village), address books, armorials.
- **Gesher Galicia** (geshergalicia.org): Galicia cadastral maps, inventories of surviving records, Jewish and Christian records (part requires membership).
- **IPN** (ipn.gov.pl) and **AAN**: 20th century, deportations, the security apparatus.
- **Lithuania**: epaveldas.lt (digitised church books, inventories; also LVIA material), LVIA (Lithuanian State Historical Archives) for metric books and revisions of the Vilnius and Kaunas governorates.
- **Latvia**: Raduraksti (raduraksti.arhivi.lv) church books, 1897 census and later censuses, free with registration.
- **Estonia**: Saaga (ais.ra.ee) church books and censuses.
- **Belarus**: NIAB (niab.by), radzima.net place pages, Zhnivo and hryc.by indexes.
- **Ukraine (Galicia, Volhynia)**: Central State Historical Archive in Lviv (TsDIAL) and oblast archives; FamilySearch films.
- **Jewish**: JewishGen Locality Database tells which records survive for each town.

## Names and places
Town names differ by language: Wilno / Vilnius / Vilna, Lwów / Lviv / Lemberg / Lvov, Grodno / Hrodna, Kowno / Kaunas. Latin forms in Catholic books (Joannes, Hedvigis), Polish forms for Ukrainian names in Galicia (Wasyl, Hryc, -czuk). Women use -ska / -cka forms in Polish, -aitė/-ienė forms in Lithuanian.

## Calendar trap
Russian Poland used Julian civil dates (often double dates in Catholic books); Galicia and Prussia used Gregorian. Greek Catholic church calendar may differ. Keep dates as written.

## Requests
Polish State Archives answer genealogical requests by e-mail (often paid); for records less than 100 years (births) or 80 years (marriages, deaths) the civil registry office (Urząd Stanu Cywilnego) holds them, available to relatives. Lithuanian Archives: LVIA and the Lithuanian State Archives accept online genealogical requests (paid).
