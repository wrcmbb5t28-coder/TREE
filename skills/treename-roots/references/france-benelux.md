# France, Belgium, Luxembourg and Monaco

Partly adapted from hermes-genealogy-skills by gtertrais (MIT).

## France
**Civil registration (état civil)**: from 1792 in every commune; before that, parish registers (registres paroissiaux) from 1539 (Villers-Cotterêts) and systematically from the 17th century, Protestant registers separate. Two copies: one at the town hall (mairie), one at the court, later deposited in the Archives départementales (AD).
**Access**: birth and marriage records are public after 75 years, death records immediately. Most AD have put civil and parish registers online free, usually up to about 1900–1920.
**Key sources**
- **Archives départementales** websites (one per département, e.g. archives.aisne.fr, archives.paris.fr): état civil, tables décennales (ten-year indexes per commune, the fastest way into a commune), registres matricules militaires (military registration of every man from about the 1860s birth cohorts, giving physical description, addresses and war service), census lists (recensements, from 1836), notarial records, land registers (cadastre).
- **FranceArchives** (francearchives.gouv.fr): national portal of archive catalogues.
- **Fichier des décès INSEE** (search through deces.matchid.io): every death in France since 1970, with birth date and place.
- **Mémoire des hommes** (memoiredeshommes.sga.defense.gouv.fr): soldiers who died in WWI ("Morts pour la France"), WWII and later conflicts; war diaries of units.
- **Base Léonore**: Legion of Honour files.
- **Gallica** (gallica.bnf.fr) and **RetroNews**: digitised newspapers and books, the Journal officiel (naturalisations, name changes).
- **Geneanet** and **Filae**: indexes and user trees (leads); Geneanet cemetery photos.
- Paris: civil records before 1860 were burned in 1871 and partly reconstituted (état civil reconstitué).
**Names**: married women appear under their maiden name in records; first names are often several (Jean Marie Joseph), the usual name may be the second one. Old handwriting and Latin in parish registers before 1792; Revolutionary calendar dates 1793–1805 (an II, vendémiaire...) must be converted.
**Requests**: for recent records ask the mairie of the place of the event (copie intégrale or extrait with filiation) through service-public.fr; descendants in direct line can get full copies of records less than 75 years old.

## Belgium
- **State Archives of Belgium** (arch.be); the online search is **AGATHA** (agatha.arch.be), parish registers before 1796 and état civil since 1796, free.
- Public access: births after 100 years, marriages after 75 years, deaths after 50 years. More recent records from the commune's civil registry (état civil / burgerlijke stand), for direct descendants.
- Population registers (registres de population, from 1846) in communal archives: whole households with moves.
- **BelgicaPress** (belgicapress.be) and KBR for newspapers. Military (milice) records are generally not online.
- Bilingual country: places and records in French or Dutch (Liège / Luik, Bruxelles / Brussel); German in the east cantons.

## Luxembourg
Civil registration from 1796 (French period); records at the National Archives (anlux.public.lu) and communes; FamilySearch has filmed civil and parish registers.

## Monaco
Archives of the Palais princier and the mairie; few online records.
