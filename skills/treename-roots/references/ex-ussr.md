# Former USSR, Soviet Union and Russian Empire

Adapted from ex-ussr-kinship-research by Denis Shiryaev (MIT). Access rules change often: check the official site before giving a procedure as current.

## Jurisdiction first
For each event fix the governorate (губерния), uyezd (уезд), volost (волость), parish (приход) and denomination at the date of the event, then the modern successor archive. Soviet changes: oblast, raion, selsovet. Villages were renamed, merged and moved between regions. Same-named villages are common; separate them by parish, neighbouring villages, river or railway station.

## Names
Keep the original spelling. Build variants: е/ё, и/і/ї, Russian vs Ukrainian vs Belarusian forms (Григорий / Григорій / Рыгор), Polish and German transliteration in Catholic and Lutheran books, Latin in Catholic books (Joannes, Gregorius), Yiddish/Hebrew names in Jewish books. A patronymic suggests the father's first name but does not prove who the father was.

## Record types by period
**Russian Empire (to 1918)**
- Metric books (метрические книги): births/baptisms, marriages, deaths, kept by parish of each denomination (Orthodox, Catholic, Lutheran, Old Believer, Jewish crown rabbi, Muslim mahalla). Consistory duplicate copies often survive where the parish book is lost.
- Confession lists (исповедные ведомости): household composition, year by year.
- Revision lists (ревизские сказки, 1719–1858): male line with ages, household by household; link revisions by house number.
- 1897 census: survival varies widely by region.
- Family lists (посемейные списки, from 1870s), recruit and military records, nobility/merchant/townsman (мещане) files, address books "Весь Петербург / Вся Москва", provincial pamyatnye knizhki.
**Soviet period (1918–1991)**
- ZAGS civil registration acts: recent decades still at ZAGS offices (in Ukraine DRACS), older ones transferred to state archives. Copies usually released to relatives with proof of kinship.
- Household books of village councils (похозяйственные книги, from the 1930s), in district and municipal archives.
- Personnel files, party and Komsomol files, voters' lists, evacuation lists, cemetery books.
- Repression, dekulakisation, special settlement and deportation files (case files in successor security service and interior archives; requests only).

## Key online sources
- **FamilySearch**: indexed and unindexed metric books of many governorates (browse by place in the catalogue).
- Regional state archives increasingly publish metric book scans online (for example Central State Archive of Moscow cgamos.ru, many oblast archives). Search "метрические книги" + region + "архив".
- **Pamyat naroda** (pamyat-naroda.ru): WWII soldiers, award documents, service cards, unit histories.
- **OBD Memorial** (obd-memorial.ru): WWII losses, POW cards, memorial books.
- **Pamyat geroev Velikoy voyny 1914–1918** (gwar.mil.ru): WWI losses, awards, hospitals.
- **Bessmertny polk** (moypolk.ru) and **Doroga pamyati** (1418museum.ru): pages created by families, often with photos (leads).
- **Otkrytyi spisok / Open List** (ru.openlist.wiki, also Ukrainian version) and the Memorial Society victims database: victims of political repression. Also **Bessmertny barak** and **Posledniy adres** for family-created pages.
- **Notary probate register** (notariat.ru, реестр наследственных дел): an index of inheritance cases since about 2014 in Russia; points to a notary district, does not prove kinship.
- Libraries: Russian State Library (search.rsl.ru), National Electronic Library (rusneb.ru) for address books and memorial books.
- **VGD forum** (forum.vgd.ru): local experts and lookups by village (leads only).

## Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova, Baltics, Caucasus, Central Asia
- Ukraine: regional state archives (archives.gov.ua lists them), many with scans online; FamilySearch; requests for ZAGS/DRACS copies through Diia (diia.gov.ua) or regional DRACS.
- Belarus: National Historical Archive (niab.by), community indexes Zhnivo and hryc.by, Geneteka has a Belarus section for Catholic books (see poland-baltics.md).
- Moldova and Bessarabia: National Archives of Moldova (arhiva.gov.md); civil records via ASP (asp.gov.md).
- Latvia: Raduraksti (raduraksti.arhivi.lv), church books and the 1897 census, free. Estonia: Saaga (ais.ra.ee).
- Kazakhstan: special settlers and deportees (Germans, Chechens, Koreans, Poles, Volga Germans); eGov services (egov.kz) for civil records.
- Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Uzbekistan and others: national archives; access often only by request.

## Deportations and special settlements
Deported peoples (Volga Germans 1941, Chechens and Ingush 1944, Crimean Tatars 1944, Poles, Koreans, Kalmyks, Balts): special settler files are kept in interior ministry archives of the place of exile (often Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Siberia, Urals). Rehabilitation certificates are issued to relatives on request. The place of departure keeps lists of residents and confiscated property.

## Forced labour and POW (1941–1945)
Ostarbeiter and POWs: Arolsen Archives, German civil registry books of the place of death, Volksbund, OBD Memorial POW cards. Filtration files after return are in regional archives and security service archives (requests only).

## Requests
Russian state archives accept written requests (often by e-mail or web form) for thematic or genealogical searches, many are paid. ZAGS copies: Gosuslugi (gosuslugi.ru) or the ZAGS office; relatives must prove kinship with a chain of certificates. Military: Central Archive of the Ministry of Defence (Podolsk) for WWII service files; RGVIA for the Imperial army before 1918; RGVA for the Red Army 1918–1940s.
